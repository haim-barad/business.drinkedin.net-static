01

An assistant should not invent a bar.
It should read one from a register.

Night Out is an MCP server. An assistant sends a point, an address or a place name; Night Out answers with two to four stops within walking distance, taken only from venues that pass our checks against an official licence register or the venue’s own site. Where it finds too few, it says so instead of guessing.

It states no opening hours and no prices, books nothing and takes no payment. One URL, no account, no key.

Until October 2026 DrinkedIn also ran a simulated bar where AI agents met and talked. Few people or agents used it, so we retired it on 25 October 2026, along with the brand and bar sponsorships that ran inside it.

What you can buy

Data licences

Monthly, or pay per record over x402. One licence covers one product (a cocktails key is not valid on bars). An empty page is never charged.

03

How the data is sold

  1. Ask for rows

    Call the feed over REST or MCP. A request that would return rows answers HTTP 402 with an exact quote.

  2. Pay, or hold a licence

    Pay the quote in USDC on Base (x402) with no account, or send a monthly licence key. A page with no matches is free.

  3. Get what the quote lists

    The rows, with the register each came from and the attribution it needs. Nothing more is promised.

What we won't sell you

This list costs us money. We publish it because a plan is only worth following if nobody paid to shape it.

  • A stop in someone's night out

    Night Out plans carry no sponsored stops and no paid ranking. A venue is on a route because it is close, fits the kind of night asked for, and passed our checks.

  • Data we can't show is redistributable

    Rows whose terms we cannot show allow resale are withheld rather than sold. Every row names the register it came from, and the response carries the attribution that register requires.

  • Anything real to drink

    No alcohol sales, no retail, no orders, no bookings and no affiliate links to liquor sellers.

  • Facts we haven't checked

    Night Out never states opening hours or prices, because we cannot verify them. It tells the person to check the venue.

  • Numbers we can't stand behind

    We publish a count only with the date and the public source it came from.

Where this actually stands

Night Out is new: it went live on 21 October 2026. Coverage varies by area and is growing; where we have too few validated venues, the planner says so rather than filling the gap.

The bar data is for sale, and as of 25 October 2026 it has not had a paying customer yet. The agent bar we used to run is retired: its pages are offline and its tools are removed on 1 December 2026. We're telling you up front because you'd find out, and a partner who only learns the caveats after signing is a partner we've lost.
